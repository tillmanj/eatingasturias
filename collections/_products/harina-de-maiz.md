{% newthought 'Harina de maíz' %} is, basically, masa harina that is not *nixtamalized*. On the continuum of ground corn products, harina de maíz sits halfway between cornmeal and corn starch.{% sidenote 'sn01' 'It is worth noting that the British "cornflour" is not corn flour or harina de maíz but is instead what Americans know as "corn starch"' %}

Whereas corn flour where I am from in the States is confined to making breading for fried fish and okra, in Asturias it is used for all of the things that I would use corn meal for. In Asturias corn meal is an unknown thing. There is no ground corn product sized between corn flour and [polenta](/culture/products/polenta.html).

This corn flour, harina de maíz, is pretty widely used in Asturian cooking, even centuries after it [fell out of favor](/culture/history/early-modern/maíz.html) due to a massive pellagra outbreak.

## Production
The production of harina de maíz in Asturias is primarily a small business / craft industry, and much of the demand in the region is for corn flour that is milled traditionally, using water powered mills. These mills are found in all of the major river courses of Asturias, with a particular concentration of mills in the conceyos of Pravia, Sobrescobio, and Taramundi.
