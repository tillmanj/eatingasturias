{% newthought 'Early Modern history' %} in Asturias is the time from the Columbian Exchange to the beginning of the Peninsular War. In a food history sense, this is the period from the invention of the modern quiche through to the Mint Julep. It covers a number of huge upheavals in culture more widely, including the expulsion of the Jews from Spain, Industrialization, Migration to the Americas, 

### Columbian Exchange
*Main article: [The Columbian Exchange](/culture/history/early-modern/columbian-exchange.html)*

1492 CE - In an attempt to reach Asia, Spanish navigator Christopher Columbus lands by accident in the Bahamas. His discovery of the New World will lead to the introduction of many new foods to Europe, among them potatoes, peppers, maize, tomatoes, pineapples, allspice, cacao, and turkey.

Try as they might, the Spanish conquistadores could not bend Mother Nature to their imperious will, and their demands that the Americas produce wheat bread and wine for their sacraments fell on the equally imperious deaf ears of nature. Instead, they learned the ways of potato, corn, pumpkin, tomato, bean, and chile pepper. Imagine Spanish food without tomatoes and pimentón! As impossible as imagining American food without the previously unknown pig!

### Thirty Year's War (1618–1648)
The Little Ice Age led to a decrease in agricultural productivity, inflating grain prices. Because most of Europe was an agricultural society at the time, this meant that the economy suffered. The economic downturn led to social, political and economic strife that contributed to the outbreak of war across Europe.[^1]

The Thirty Years’ War coincided with the harshest period of the Little Ice Age, from 1560 to 1660.

### The Indianos
*Main article: [The Indianos](/culture/history/early-modern/indianos.html)*

Indianos is the name given to Asturians who went to the Americas to seek their fortunes in the 19th and early 20th centuries. They are responsible, in part, for one of the biggest developments in Asturian food distribution. Namely, the mass bottling of cider for export.

#### Argentina
Asturias gained a couple of interesting additions to its food culture from the Indianos as well. A man known as Anton de los Pontones who had been a gaucho in Argentina brought back the technique of cooking beef a la estaca, and adapted it to lamb. There is still a yearly festival de cordero estaca near where he was from.
